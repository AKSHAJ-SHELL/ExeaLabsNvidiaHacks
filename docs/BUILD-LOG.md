# Build log

Deviations from the contract, one paragraph each.

**Family interleave in sampling.** The contract says to shuffle the task product with `random.Random(0)` and cycle. With 192 tasks, the first 4 shuffled tasks were all `counter_to_counter`, which fails the smoke bar (each family must be in the 4-episode run). `arrival.tasks.propose.shuffled` still shuffles with `Random(0)`, then interleaves the two families while keeping the shuffled order within each one. The miss pattern still sits on attempt indexes 9, 19, 29, …

**Sidecar path.** The contract's `embodiment.json` example gives `cartesian_sidecar: "../hero_kitchen.cartesian.jsonl"` relative to `meta/`, but it also places the sidecar at `out/datasets/<room>.cartesian.jsonl`, outside the dataset folder. The writer records `../../hero_kitchen.cartesian.jsonl`, the path that actually resolves from `meta/`. A test checks that it resolves.

**Validator exit code.** The contract's validator section says "Exits 0 when a required file is missing or a slice is wrong", which contradicts the exit codes in 05-repo-map.md. The validator exits 1 in those cases.

**`--from-roomplan` objects (superseded, see below).** RoomPlan does not capture small objects, so the RoomPlan bundle has none and no tasks can be proposed from it alone. The first version borrowed the object placements from `scans/<room>/scan.json` when that file existed. The twin is smaller, with only the sink plus a synthesized counter. If the counter inset rejects the scanned pose, the seed falls back to the lowest surviving start pose.

**Timing.** On an Apple Silicon laptop, `demo --episodes 1000 --videos sparse` takes about 1 s for generation and export (1,111 attempts, 111 dropped, 8 mp4s). `--videos all` for 1,000 episodes was not needed and was not timed.

**Real RoomPlan exports.** The first normalizer assumed plain-string categories and a floor at ARKit y = 0. Real exports (2 public ones now in `tests/fixtures/roomplan/`, sources in its README) use keyed enums (`{"chair": {}}`), camelCase (`washerDryer`), a `confidence` enum, a floor around y = -1.5, zero wall thickness, and arbitrary room rotation. `arrival.scan.roomplan` now unwraps enums, snake-cases categories, drops `low` confidence, subtracts the floor height, gives zero-thickness walls 0.1 m, rotates the room so the longest wall runs along +X, moves the wall bounding box to (0, 0), and snaps yaws within 5° of 90° multiples. Tests check that both real exports normalize, compile, rest on the floor, and form closed wall loops.

**Placed objects instead of borrowed ones.** `--from-roomplan` no longer copies objects from the authored scan. `arrival.twin.compile.place_default_objects(twin, seed)` puts a mug and a bowl (catalog sizes) on the counter nearest a sink (synthesized or scanned; dining tables last), rejecting spots over a sink or stove, with `random.Random(seed)`. Objects carry `placed_by: "place_default_objects"`. The proposer now picks the counter that holds objects as the source. Neither public real export contains a kitchen fixture, so they yield 0 tasks; a synthetic kitchen in Apple's encoding (rotated 30°, floor at -1.4) is the test that yields both families.

**Synthesized counter length.** A counter synthesized around a sink or stove is now `max(width + 1.2, 1.8)` m long (was `max(width + 0.4, 1.2)`), so there is room beside the sink to place objects. It inherits the fixture's yaw, with its long axis along the fixture's local X.

**Yaw-aware geometry.** `in_top_rect`, `_rect_distance` and so `on_counter` transform points into the fixture's local frame (`arrival.twin.compile.to_local`). In `layout.yaml`, axis-aligned walls keep their swapped-size convention; oblique walls get `size = [length, thickness, height]` plus `z_rot` from the wall direction.

**`--scans` default.** A relative `--scans` that does not exist in the cwd resolves against the repo root (`arrival.cli.resolve_scans`). A missing room or a missing `roomplan.json` exits with a message naming the path.

**Upload hardening.** `POST /jobs` streams the body to a temp file in 1 MB chunks, then finds the `scan` part by boundary scanning over an mmap (no `email` parser, no full in-memory copy). A body shorter than `Content-Length`, a missing closing boundary, an empty file, or no filename returns 400. The handler has a 30 s socket timeout. The room page links `out/datasets/<room>_sim` when it exists and shows its `arrival_generator`, `arrival_physically_valid`, `total_episodes` and families (`arrival_families` or `families`).

**`.env` for Token Factory.** `arrival.models.token_factory.config()` reads `.env` from the cwd or repo root with a small stdlib parser, only when `TOKEN_FACTORY_API_KEY` is unset, and never overrides set variables. Nothing else loads `.env`.

**Scope change: MuJoCo allowed.** The project lead overrode docs/01-decisions.md: MuJoCo is now an optional `sim` extra and the scope includes simulation with drawers, doors and moving people (`arrival/sim/`, built separately). README documents it and lists the remaining gaps.

- Integrator: sim drawer on counter_a was blocked by the closed cabinet door panel overlapping the drawer stack; door width now shrinks to clear the drawer (`door_w`), open_drawer 8/8. Cabinet door hinge axis flipped so it swings outward (it previously rotated into the carcass); open_door is still 0% (IK/reach), not claimed.

**MAN-1: Panda look and motion.** The arm keeps the Franka kinematic chain and its capsule collision links, now hidden (`group="3"`) under visual-only Panda geometry (`contype="0" conaffinity="0"`): white link housings with dark joint rings, a low link0 base on a pedestal plate, and a Franka-style hand. Contact physics is unchanged. `ik.solve` adds a nullspace pull toward `robot.HOME` and only stops once the posture has settled, so the elbow stays up between waypoints; the worst counter_to_sink IK error dropped from 0.080 m to 0.012 m. `ScriptedExpert` uses whichever of the two equivalent parallel-jaw yaws (yaw or yaw + π) is closer to the hand's heading, so the drawer grasp no longer spins joint 7 by about 3.3 rad. Waypoints play with min-jerk timing, and the six drawer-pull waypoints form one via-point move instead of six stop-and-go pulses. Steps per segment are unchanged, so success and frame counts match the old run: counter_to_sink 8/8 (1408 frames), open_drawer 8/8 (1168 frames). open_door stays 0/4 (MAN-2).

**SIM-1: Kitchen visuals.** The `hero_kitchen` twin puts the fridge over `counter_a` and the stove over `counter_b` (TWN-6), so the old scene drew counter_b's drawers inside the stove, with their fronts z-fighting the stove's front, and counter_b's top over the burners. Counters now yield to appliances: `_span` trims a counter's carcass and top where an end runs into a fridge or stove footprint, and `drawer_x` treats a stove like a fridge, so counter_b's drawers move from inside the stove to the middle of the counter (the robot base follows). Those are the only physics changes. Everything else is visual-only (`contype="0" conaffinity="0"`; the physical drawer and door panels, handle standoffs, freezer panel and burner boxes are kept but hidden in group 3): recessed fronts with 3 mm reveals and slim steel bar handles on standoffs, a dark backer that closes the open band under the countertop and forms the toe-kick, a 1.5 cm countertop overhang (the fronts move back; the top's edge stays put because the wrist passes about 9 mm from it in open_drawer), a backsplash, a sink rim, drain and faucet, a gas range with a cooktop, four burners, knobs and an oven door, and a fridge with a fixed freezer door and handles on standoffs. counter_to_sink 8/8 (1408 frames) and open_drawer 8/8 (1168 frames) are unchanged.

**SIM-3: More moving parts.** The stove's oven door, which was a painted-on front, is now a bottom-hinged body with a handle bar and a site (`stove_1_oven_door`), the faucet grew a lever on a hinge (`sink_1_faucet`), each counter run carries wall cabinets with one hinged door per 0.6 m (`counter_a_upper_door0..2`, `counter_b_upper_door0..1`), and two fixture types that the twin already knew but the scene dropped are built: a floor-standing dishwasher with a bottom-hinged door (a counter run whose end overlaps it is trimmed back, the same rule as for a fridge or stove; an appliance wholly inside a run is still not cut out, which matters for a dishwasher beside a sink under a synthesized counter and is left for the twin side) and a counter-top microwave with a side-hinged door. `part_names(twin)` lists every joint with its handle site and an open position, and a test drives each one open, checks the handle moved, lets physics run and checks nothing blew up. Two things came out of measuring instead of guessing. First, wall cabinets at the usual 1.40 m bottom edge broke counter_to_sink outright (0/2): the wrist links reach 1.50 m over the back of the counter while dropping into the sink, so the cabinets start at 1.56 m. Second, a bottom-hinged door balanced on its hinge falls open on its own; the panel now sits 8 mm behind the hinge line so gravity holds it shut, and a test lets the scene rest for 300 steps and asserts every part is still closed. The wall-cabinet run stops 3 cm short of each end so it stays inside the 5 cm wall boxes. A drawer row above the cabinet door bay was left out: its front would sit inside the MAN-2 door panel's collision box and move the handle that MAN-2 was tuned on. Objects now carry `conaffinity="3"` so they rest on drawer trays and open oven doors (the same change MAN-5 made for put_in_drawer). The hero kitchen's rates are unchanged (counter_to_sink 3/3, open_drawer 3/3, open_door 0/2 on main's policy), and with MAN-2 and MAN-5 merged on top every family runs: 24/24 across seven families with two people, 78 tests.

**SIM-4: More objects.** The object catalog (`arrival/catalog/objects.py`) now holds a spec per id: shape, nominal size, mass at that size, friction and colour, for mug, cup, bowl, plate, sponge, apple, bottle (body plus neck), pan (open ring plus handle) and spatula. Mass scales with volume, so a half-height mug weighs half; the mug's visual handle weighs nothing (it quietly added 10 g until a test caught it). `place_default_objects` puts a mug, bowl, plate, cup and apple on a RoomPlan counter, each at a seeded size within 8% of nominal, and skips one that does not fit. `PICKABLE` is the set the expert holds by the body: the cup, apple and bottle go counter to sink 4/4 each with no policy change; the spatula does not leave the counter (0/4 in both orientations) because at 1 cm thick it sits under the grasp height the expert uses, so it stays scenery. The bowl's hollow rim-grasp shape belongs to MAN-4.
